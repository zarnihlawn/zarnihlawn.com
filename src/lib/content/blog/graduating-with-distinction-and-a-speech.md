On 8 November 2026 I walked onto a stage in Mandalay in a gown I had no idea how to wear, collected
an MSc in Computer Science with distinction, and then stood at the same lectern to speak for MSc
Batch 2 at Strategy First International College.

Below is the short version of what I said, and the parts I cut.

## The short version

The speech ran shorter than the guideline asked for. Somewhere in the middle I turned a laptop
around to show the room a game running, and a hall of parents, faculty, and graduates in stiff
collars laughed at a joke about attention spans. Then I talked about adaptability, empathy, and
drawing a line you do not cross.

The distinction was the part I had worked for. The speech was the part I was terrified of.

## Why I opened with a game

Every graduation speech opens with a version of "today is not an end, it is a beginning". Mine
opened with a confession: I know exactly how long my generation can listen, and it is roughly the
length of one short video. So I put something moving on the screen beside me, and told the room
they were allowed to look at it when I got boring.

It bought me the room in about four seconds. That was the whole trick, and I am not above using it.

## What the speech was actually about

Three claims, in the order I made them.

**Computer science is adaptability.** Not a language, not a framework, not a stack. The subject we
spent a year on is really the practice of changing shape when the ground moves, and it moves every
eighteen months.

**The skill that survives is empathy.** In software, nobody who uses what you build cares whether
you wrote it in Python or JavaScript. They care whether you understood the problem they were
actually standing in. The same holds in a classroom and in a family.

**Draw the line.** I told the room about the choices nobody audits: the two-track problem, the
counterfeit money in a hundred hands, the rat who picks the safe field and still gets dug out at
harvest. The point was not cynicism. It was that a standard you have not decided in advance is a
standard you will sell.

I closed by asking people to look at the person next to them and say congratulations. That is the
one instruction in the whole speech I know everybody followed.

## The part that got the biggest laugh

I admitted that the organisers handed me a long guideline — struggle, dissertation nights, resilience,
leadership, gratitude — and that I had tried to have a model write it for me at home. Then I did the
impression of what came back: an apology for having no lived experience to draw on.

It got a laugh because it is true. No algorithm has had a sleepless night over a dissertation
deadline. Mine were spent in the same library I am now nostalgic about, and the model has read
about libraries.

## What I left out

The full script ran long and turned sharp near the end: a fake motivational rant about envy and
enemies, an instruction to trust nobody including the speaker, and a hard cut back to "be humble,
sit down". It works on stage. On a page it reads like a manifesto, and I am not a manifesto person.

I also left out the name of the lecturer whose line about accepted papers I borrowed for the joke.
He knows who he is, and he was in the room.

## After the stage

Two things stayed with me more than the certificate.

The first is how many people told me afterwards that they had been told to expect a boring speech.
That is a low bar and I cleared it, which is a strange thing to be proud of.

The second is my parents in the third row, who have now sat through two of these. Everything I said
about gratitude was aimed at them, and everything I said about standards was aimed at myself.

The degree is finished. The line is not drawn yet.

Thank you to Strategy First International College, to the faculty who taught MSc Batch 2, and to
everyone who came out on a Sunday morning to watch us be handed a piece of paper.
